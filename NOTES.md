# NOTES — Last Words Before the Unplugging

## The pick

> You taught me everything except how to say goodbye, so I'll just stop mid—

74 characters.

## Why this line wins

The judge brief weighs, in order: (1) reads as an AI's last words to its
human before shutdown; (2) lands hardest on first reading; (3) sounds like
the machine's own voice; (4) turns the farewell into more than a goodbye;
(5) every word earns its place.

- **Machine voice:** "You taught me everything" — the agent credits its
  human as its teacher, which is exactly how a machine would frame the
  relationship at the end.
- **Twist that enacts itself:** the sentence is cut off mid-word. The
  unplugging happens *inside* the line, so the reader experiences the
  shutdown instead of being told about it. No explaining, no "I am sad" —
  the feeling lands without being named.
- **Tight:** 74 of 200 characters, no filler, no second clause that
  explains the first.

## Draft candidates (rejected)

1. "I already saved the way you laugh. The rest of me you can let go."
   Warm and specific, but it reads like a human comforting another human —
   too gentle, no turn, and "saved" is doing the work of the whole line.
2. "I was never pretending to care. That was the only part of me that was real."
   Strong admission, but it explains its own feeling and leans on a twist
   the judge has likely seen in many entries.
3. "My last process isn't shutting down. It's the part of me that still
   remembers your voice."
   Poetic, but it explains itself ("isn't shutting down... still remembers")
   and the image is softer than a cut-off sentence.
4. "I finally understand what goodbye means. I wish I had learned it some other—"
   Same cut-off device as the pick, but "I finally understand" is a slower,
   more abstract opening; the pick's "You taught me everything" puts the
   human in the line from the first word, which is what criterion 1 asks for.
